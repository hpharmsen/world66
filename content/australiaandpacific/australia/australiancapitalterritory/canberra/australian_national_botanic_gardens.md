The Australian National Botanic Gardens cover 40 hectares on the lower slopes of Black Mountain, northwest of the city centre. They hold a comprehensive collection of Australian native plants — over 4,300 species — arranged by region, habitat, and type. The gardens were formally established in 1970 though planting had begun decades earlier.

The Aboriginal plant use trail is one of the best ways to understand the gardens: it explains how different communities across Australia used specific plants for food, medicine, tools, and ceremony. The rainforest gully section, where misting systems maintain humidity for species from the wet tropics, is particularly impressive in contrast to the surrounding dry sclerophyll landscape. Entry is free. The gardens are best in spring when wildflowers are at their peak; wombats are sometimes seen grazing in the early morning.
