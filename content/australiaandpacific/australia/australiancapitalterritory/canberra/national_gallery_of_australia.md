The National Gallery of Australia opened in 1982 on the southern shore of Lake Burley Griffin, in a building designed by Colin Madigan. The collection of around 166,000 works is the largest in Australia and covers Australian art from the colonial period to the present, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander art, Asian and Pacific art, and a solid European and American holding. Admission to the permanent collection is free.

The Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander galleries are a particular strength — the collection includes bark paintings, sculpture, fibre work, and video art, representing communities across the continent. The garden sculpture walk around the building includes pieces by Rodin and Henry Moore. The gallery also runs major ticketed special exhibitions, which regularly draw queues. Allow two to three hours for the permanent collection alone.
