The old bazaar, or çarshia, runs through the heart of the walled city and was once one of the most active commercial centres in central Albania. Craftsmen, merchants, and traders gathered here from the Ottoman period through to the 20th century, and the physical layout — covered passages, small workshops opening directly onto the lane, arched doorways — is largely intact even if many of the individual trades have changed.

Today the bazaar is a mixed-use area: some traditional metalworkers and cobblers remain alongside tea houses and small grocery stalls. It is not a tourist market in any sense — it functions as a real neighbourhood shopping street. The Ethnographic Museum is at the heart of the bazaar quarter and makes a natural anchor for a walk through the area.
