The Shrine of Hazrat Ali — universally known as the Blue Mosque — is Mazar-i-Sharif's defining building and one of the most beautiful religious structures in all of Central Asia. The tilework shifts between turquoise, cobalt, and deep lapis depending on the light and the angle: in the early morning the facade glows with an intensity that is difficult to photograph and impossible to forget. The shrine was built by the Timurid rulers in the late 15th century and restored and expanded repeatedly since.

The courtyard is home to a famous flock of white doves, considered sacred by pilgrims, who believe that any grey dove that joins the flock turns white within forty days through the shrine's blessing. At Nowruz — the Persian New Year in March — the courtyard fills with tens of thousands of pilgrims for the Jahenda Bala ceremony, when a sacred flag is raised over the shrine. The complex is a working pilgrimage site and functions accordingly: visitors are welcome but should dress conservatively and behave with respect.
