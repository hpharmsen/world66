The National Museum of Australia opened in 2001 on the Acton Peninsula, connected to the city centre by a footbridge across Lake Burley Griffin. The building itself is a work of postmodern architecture — a tangle of intersecting geometries designed by Ashton Raggatt McDougall — that divides opinion sharply. The collection inside is less controversial and covers the full span of Australian history from the deep past to the recent.

The permanent galleries include one of the most comprehensive collections of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander material culture in the country, along with exhibits covering the gold rush, immigration, the environment, and recent social history. The museum is free to enter and generally well-attended, though it sees fewer visitors than the War Memorial. The loop road around the Acton Peninsula makes for a good evening walk with views across to the Parliamentary Triangle.
