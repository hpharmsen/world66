The National Portrait Gallery opened in its permanent building in 2008, next to Old Parliament House in the Parliamentary Triangle. The collection holds over 3,000 portraits of Australians who have shaped the country — politicians, artists, scientists, athletes, activists, and others — in media ranging from oil painting to photography, sculpture, and video. Entry is free.

The gallery's approach is more interesting than the genre of portrait painting sometimes suggests: the emphasis is on the subject and their significance rather than purely on artistic technique, which means the biographical context provided for each work is detailed and readable. The collection includes portraits by notable artists including Sidney Nolan, Arthur Boyd, and Albert Tucker, as well as photographic portraits by documentary photographers. The building is attractively sited with garden views across to the lake.
