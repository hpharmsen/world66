The Armash fish ponds occupy the flat southwestern corner of Ararat Marz, near the Turkish and Azerbaijani borders. They were built as commercial aquaculture during the Soviet period, and they have become — entirely by accident — one of the richest bird habitats in Armenia. The brackish marshes sit at the intersection of three global migration flyways, and over a million waterbirds pass through annually, with over 200 species recorded at the site.

Regular visitors include purple herons, spoonbills, pygmy cormorants, various egret species, and the globally endangered white-headed duck. During spring and autumn migration, the diversity can be overwhelming. The area lacks formal visitor infrastructure, which keeps it quiet even during peak migration. Early mornings in April and May are the best time; access is by car from the town of Ararat on the road toward the border village of Pokr Vedi.
