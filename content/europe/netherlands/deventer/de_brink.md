  In the Middle Ages, De Brink was one of the largest market squares in the northern Netherlands. Five annual fairs were held here from 1386 onward, attracting traders from across the Hanseatic network. The square's scale — unusual even by Dutch standards — reflects the commercial ambitions of a city that was briefly one of the most important trading hubs in northern Europe.

De Brink is Deventer's central square and its beating heart. It is exceptionally large by Dutch standards, wide enough that the buildings on either side feel like walls of a courtyard rather than a cramped medieval street. The facades that line it range from 15th-century stepped gables to Renaissance stone frontages, with the Waag — the 1528 weigh house, now Museum de Waag — anchoring the centre of the square.

On Saturday mornings a colourful market fills the square with fruit, cheese, flowers, and the general sociable noise of a Dutch weekend. In August, De Brink becomes the epicentre of the Deventer Boekenmarkt, when more than 850 book stalls extend for six kilometres through the surrounding streets, attracting over 100,000 visitors. It is one of the best reasons to visit Deventer, and one of the more pleasant ways to spend a Sunday in the Netherlands.
