Parliament House was opened by Queen Elizabeth II on 9 May 1988 — the bicentenary of European settlement in Australia — after more than a decade of construction. The American architect Romaldo Giurgola won the design competition with a scheme that embedded the building into Capital Hill rather than sitting on top of it: the grass-covered roof becomes a public walkway, and the building reads as part of the landscape from most angles. It replaced the temporary Old Parliament House, which had served as the seat of government since 1927.

The interior is open to the public and free to enter. Both chambers — the Senate and the House of Representatives — can be viewed from the public galleries when parliament is in session. The building contains an extraordinary collection of Australian art and craft, including a 20-metre-long tapestry based on an Arthur Boyd painting, a mosaic forecourt designed by Aboriginal artist Michael Nelson Jagamara, and countless other works commissioned for the building. The Great Hall and Marble Foyer are architecturally impressive even without the art. Allow two to three hours.
