The Basilica di Sant'Antonio — known simply as "Il Santo" in Padova — is one of the great pilgrimage churches of Italy, attracting millions of visitors each year from across the Catholic world. The building itself is a striking mixture of styles: a Byzantine-domed exterior, a Gothic nave, and a Renaissance-era high altar with bronze reliefs by Donatello that are among the finest works of their kind.

St Anthony died in Padova in 1231 and was canonised within a year — an almost unprecedented speed. His tomb in the Cappella del Santo is the focus of pilgrimage and is surrounded by marble reliefs depicting miracles. The church is vast and free to enter. The Treasury with the relics requires a separate entrance. Outside, Donatello's equestrian statue of the Venetian condottiere Gattamelata (1453) stands in the piazza — the first full-scale bronze equestrian statue of the Renaissance.
