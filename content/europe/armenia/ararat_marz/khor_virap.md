Khor Virap — Armenian for "deep pit" — sits on a low hill at the Turkish border, framed by the double peaks of Mount Ararat rising enormous on the far side. Few sights in the South Caucasus match the combination: a working pilgrimage monastery of honey-coloured stone with an extinct volcanic cone, still in Turkey, dominating the sky behind it. The monastery is eight kilometres south of modern Artashat, at the site of ancient Artaxata.

The complex comprises the 17th-century Church of the Holy Mother of God and an older Chapel of St Gregory, beneath which lies the dungeon — the khor virap itself — where Gregory the Illuminator was imprisoned before his miraculous release and the conversion of Armenia. A ladder descends into the pit; it is a tight, atmospheric descent worth making. The surrounding walls offer an unobstructed panorama across the Ararat plain to the mountain.
