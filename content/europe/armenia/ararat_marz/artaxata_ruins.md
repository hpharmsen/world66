Ancient Artaxata — Artashat in Armenian — was founded around 176 BC by King Artaxias I on a peninsula of nine hills at the confluence of the Araks and Metsamor rivers. It became the largest and most prosperous city of the Armenian kingdom, admired by Strabo and later sacked and burned by the Roman general Gnaeus Domitius Corbulo in 58 AD. The city recovered and served as Armenia's capital until the court moved to Dvin in the 4th century.

The ruins occupy the hills immediately surrounding Khor Virap monastery, where earthen ramparts and sections of wall remain visible. The full extent of ancient Artaxata — a city of some 200,000 people at its height — ran across an area now largely under cultivation. Archaeological excavations begun in 1970 have uncovered mosaic floors, terracotta tiles, and imported Hellenistic ceramics, attesting to a city thoroughly integrated into the Mediterranean trading world.
