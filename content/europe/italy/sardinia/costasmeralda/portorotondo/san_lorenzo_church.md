San Lorenzo Church sits on the hill above Porto Rotondo's harbour, reached by a short climb from the piazza. It was designed by Andrea Cascella and built in the 1960s as part of the original resort development — an unusual modernist building with a flat, slightly wedge-shaped facade and rough stone walls that blend into the Sardinian landscape.

The interior is the reason to visit: Mario Ceroli's carved olive-wood installation fills the entire space, from the pews to the altar to the large sculptural figure of the ascending Christ. The effect is warm, otherworldly, and entirely unlike a conventional church. It holds Sunday mass in summer and is otherwise open to visitors during daylight hours.
