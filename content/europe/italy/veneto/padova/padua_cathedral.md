Padova's cathedral is a large 16th-century building with an unfinished facade — the original design, attributed to Michelangelo, was never fully executed and the front remains bare stone. The interior is spacious and relatively plain by Italian standards, but the adjacent Baptistery is the reason to come to this end of the historic centre.

The Baptistery, a Romanesque building from the 12th century, is entirely covered in 14th-century frescoes by Giusto de' Menabuoi — a cycle of Old and New Testament scenes with a magnificent Christ Pantocrator in the dome that is among the finest Byzantine-influenced fresco programs in northern Italy. It is often overlooked in favour of the Scrovegni Chapel but is very nearly as impressive for visitors who appreciate medieval painting.
