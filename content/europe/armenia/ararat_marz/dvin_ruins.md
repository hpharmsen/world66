Dvin was the capital of medieval Armenia from around the 4th century until repeated Arab raids left it ruined in the 13th century — nearly a thousand years as the administrative, ecclesiastical, and commercial heart of the Armenian world. Today the site consists of earthworks, foundation walls, and scattered excavation trenches spread across agricultural land near the villages of Verin Dvin and Hnaberd, about 35 kilometres south of Yerevan.

The excavations, ongoing since 1937, have produced an exceptional record of Armenian urban life through the early medieval period: ironwork, copperware, ceramics, and architectural fragments now housed in Yerevan's History Museum. On the ground, the scale of the ancient city is most legible from the earthen ramparts at the perimeter. The site lacks signage and facilities, but its archaeological significance — Dvin produced the earliest known example of Armenian manuscript production — makes it worthwhile for anyone serious about Armenian history.
