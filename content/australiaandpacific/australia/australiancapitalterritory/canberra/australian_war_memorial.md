The Australian War Memorial, opened in 1941, is one of the largest and most comprehensive war museums in the world. It combines a national shrine — where the Roll of Honour lists the names of over 102,000 Australians who died in war — with an extensive museum covering every military conflict in which Australia has participated, from the Boer War through to recent operations in Afghanistan and Iraq. The Hall of Memory at the centre contains the Tomb of the Unknown Australian Soldier.

The collection includes aircraft, tanks, artillery, personal equipment, diaries, letters, and an enormous archive of photographs and film. The recently expanded Anzac Hall houses full-scale aircraft including a Lancaster bomber and a Spitfire. The memorial is free and takes a full day to do properly; most visitors give it two or three hours, which covers the main galleries without exhausting them. The Sculpture Garden outside contains a powerful collection of commemorative works.
