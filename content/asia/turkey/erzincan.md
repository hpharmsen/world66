Erzincan is a city in eastern Anatolia, set in a broad valley between mountain ranges. It has been repeatedly devastated by earthquakes — most recently in 1992 — which is why the modern city looks newer than its history suggests. What survived, or was rebuilt, includes the [Mama Hatun Kümbeti](/asia/turkey/erzincan/mama_hatun_kumbet) — a distinctive Seljuk tomb with a conical roof, in the nearby town of Tercan — and the ruins of [Altıntepe](/asia/turkey/erzincan/altintepe), a significant Urartian archaeological site about 20 kilometres east of the city.

The [Gürlevik Waterfall](/asia/turkey/erzincan/girlevik_waterfall), about 30 kilometres southeast, is worth a stop if you're passing through. The surrounding mountains offer trekking in summer and attract ski tourers in winter, and the Euphrates river flowing through the region provides opportunities for whitewater rafting. Paragliding has become popular from the hills above the valley.

Erzincan sits on the North Anatolian Fault — the major tectonic boundary that has caused most of Turkey's worst earthquakes — which runs through the region and is part of what gives the landscape its particular geological drama.

The city is a transport junction for eastern Anatolia, with bus connections to Erzurum, Trabzon, Sivas, and further west. There's an airport with connections to Istanbul.
