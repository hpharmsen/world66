Museum de Waag occupies the 1528 weigh house at the centre of De Brink — one of the oldest weigh houses in the Netherlands built specifically as such. The building served as the official weighing and quality-checking station for all goods traded at Deventer's five annual Hanseatic fairs, a function it performed for centuries before its conversion to a museum.

The collection tells the story of Deventer's medieval and early modern prosperity: Hanseatic trade networks, the city's role as a centre of early book printing (Deventer was among the first Dutch cities to have a printing press, in the 1470s), and the intellectual life of the Brethren of the Common Life, the religious movement that educated Erasmus and Thomas à Kempis here. The building itself — stone-built, high-ceilinged, with deep window recesses — is as interesting as its contents.
