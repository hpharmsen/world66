  painted with an understanding of three-dimensional space and human emotion
  that was entirely new in Western art.

The Scrovegni Chapel is widely considered the starting point of Western painting. Giotto's 1303-1305 frescoes — 38 panels covering the entire interior — represent the first time in medieval art that figures look, feel, and move like real people rather than symbolic icons. The blue ceiling, the stone-grey architecture of the depicted buildings, and the faces of the figures are so familiar from reproductions that standing inside is disorienting: it is smaller and more intimate than you expect.

Visits are strictly timed — 15 minutes only, preceded by 15 minutes in a decompression chamber to stabilise humidity. Book well in advance, especially for summer. The chapel is incorporated into the Musei Civici agli Eremitani, and the combined ticket includes access to the archaeological museum and the medieval and modern art collections, which are excellent and often overlooked.
