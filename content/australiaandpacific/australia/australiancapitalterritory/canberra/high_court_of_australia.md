The High Court of Australia occupies a large, visually bold building on the shores of Lake Burley Griffin, designed by the Canberra firm Edwards Madigan Torzillo and Briggs and opened in 1980. The building's concrete and glass exterior — grand, somewhat forbidding — is appropriately serious for Australia's supreme judicial body. The public forecourt faces the lake and the National Gallery of Australia next door.

The interior can be visited during court sitting days and on weekdays when court is not in session. The great public hall is the main attraction: a six-storey space hung with a tapestry and displaying the three original courtrooms. The High Court makes decisions that shape Australian constitutional law, and a visit during a sitting day — when advocates argue cases before the justices — is genuinely interesting for anyone curious about how the law works. Entry is free.
