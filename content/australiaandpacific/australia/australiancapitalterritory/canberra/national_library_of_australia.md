The National Library of Australia occupies a neoclassical building on the western shore of Lake Burley Griffin, completed in 1968. It holds over ten million items — books, maps, manuscripts, photographs, newspapers, and digital collections — and serves as the legal deposit library for all Australian publications. It is a working reference library as much as a visitor attraction.

The public areas include a permanent exhibition space with rotating displays of items from the collection, ranging from early colonial manuscripts to photography and rare maps. The library's reading rooms are open to the public. The building itself is worth a look: the exterior colonnade and the mosaic-tiled entrance hall are among the more dignified pieces of mid-century civic architecture in Canberra.
