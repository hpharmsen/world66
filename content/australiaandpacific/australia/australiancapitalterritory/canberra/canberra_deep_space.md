The Canberra Deep Space Communication Complex at Tidbinbilla, about 40 kilometres southwest of the city, is one of three stations in NASA's Deep Space Network — the communication system that talks to every spacecraft beyond Earth orbit. The site currently maintains contact with missions ranging from the Voyager probes (now over 20 billion kilometres from Earth) to the James Webb Space Telescope and the Mars rovers.

The visitor centre is free and genuinely impressive. The 70-metre antenna — the largest steerable dish in the southern hemisphere — is visible from the approach road and puts the scale of the operation into context. The exhibition explains deep space communication in accessible terms and includes historical artefacts from the Apollo era. The surrounding Tidbinbilla Nature Reserve is worth combining into the visit: it has kangaroos, wallabies, platypuses (in the creek), and several species of birds of prey, all within easy reach of the visitor centre.
