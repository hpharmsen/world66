Caffè Pedrocchi is one of Italy's most historically significant cafés — a neoclassical building from 1831 that served for over a century as the social centre of Padova's intellectual life. The ground floor coffee rooms are still operational, and the white marble tables, dark wood panelling, and high ceilings remain largely intact.

The upper floors contain a small museum about the 1848 revolutionary period and a series of ornate rooms in different historicist styles — Egyptian, Etruscan, Greek, Renaissance, Moorish — used for events and visible on a tour. The café is at the corner of the University district, two minutes from the main university courtyard. A coffee here costs more than at the bars around the corner, but the setting is worth it once.
