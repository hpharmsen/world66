Old Parliament House served as Australia's seat of government from 1927 to 1988, making it the building where most of the defining moments of 20th-century Australian political history occurred: the dismissal of the Whitlam government, debates over the Vietnam War, the post-war immigration programme, and decades of budget nights and prime ministerial press conferences. The building is now the Museum of Australian Democracy, which uses the original chambers, offices, and corridors to tell the story of Australia's political system.

The Senate and House of Representatives chambers have been preserved as they were in 1988, complete with furniture, fittings, and the general atmosphere of a mid-century parliament. The prime ministerial suite — used by everyone from Curtin to Hawke — is another highlight. The museum is thoughtful and well-curated; it avoids the hagiographic tone that similar institutions sometimes fall into. Entry fees apply.
