The village of 2 Mai — named after the date in May when the Ottoman fleet once anchored here — sits 5 km north of Vama Veche on a low coastal bluff, and it has always attracted a different crowd. While Vama Veche got the students and the late-night parties, 2 Mai drew the writers, intellectuals, and painters who wanted the same isolation from mainstream Romania but preferred quiet evenings and conversation to amplified music.

Today both villages have commercialised considerably, but 2 Mai remains the calmer of the two. There are guesthouses, a couple of good fish restaurants, and a small beach below the village. It is an easy walk from Vama Veche along the coast path, and many people base themselves here and walk south for the evening.
