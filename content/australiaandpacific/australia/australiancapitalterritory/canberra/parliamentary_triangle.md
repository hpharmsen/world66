The Parliamentary Triangle is the ceremonial heart of Canberra, defined by the three avenues — Commonwealth, Kings, and Constitution — that meet at Parliament House on Capital Hill. Within this triangle sit almost all of the city's major national institutions: Parliament House itself, Old Parliament House, the National Gallery, the National Portrait Gallery, Questacon, the High Court, and the National Library, all arranged around Lake Burley Griffin. Burley Griffin designed the triangle so that the three apexes — Capital Hill, the War Memorial on Mount Ainslie, and City Hill in Civic — would align along deliberate axes.

Most visitors to Canberra spend the majority of their time within or just outside this triangle. The institutions are within walking distance of each other along the lakeshore, and the area is well served by cycling infrastructure. The triangle rewards slow exploration on foot — the public art, the memorial spaces, and the architecture all repay attention beyond the obvious attractions.
